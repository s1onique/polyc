EVIDENCE_KIND=r0-definition

R0 -- STATE_BOUNDARY_COST (definition; this ACT introduces the metric)

  R0=0  bytes/scalars/enums only
       The candidate consumes integers, characters, or bytes and produces
       integers, characters, or bytes. No host pointers, no records
       larger than 8 bytes. Examples: int add(int,int), int is_space(char).

  R0=1  immutable copied token/value records; no host identity required
       The candidate consumes small (PoD-like) records whose bytes are
       copied in. The records are immutable for the duration of the call.
       Examples: classify a Lexeme by tk_type + i64.

  R0=2  bounded copied semantic record; deterministic reconstruction
       possible; no raw host graph identity required
       The candidate consumes a bounded record (e.g. a small AST node
       snapshot or a flat descriptor) and produces a deterministic
       value. No raw Cctrl*/Ast*/Map*/List* identity is required for
       correctness; the host graph may be read by field but the host
       graph is not mutated.

  R0=3  opaque host handle needed but candidate itself does not mutate
       referenced graph
       The candidate must consult or interpret host graph state but only
       through read access. Examples: read cc->localenv without writing
       to it; iterate Map* without inserting.

  R0=4  candidate mutates host-owned graph/state whose identity matters
       The candidate writes to host graph nodes by identity (e.g.
       appends to body->stms with prev-pointer arithmetic).

  R0=5  candidate owns or depends on execution-context/recovery authority,
       mutable callback authority, or non-local control transfer
       The candidate installs/restores jmp_buf, owns longjmp authority,
       mutates cc->current_recovery, holds a callback table, or routes
       control flow through opaque host handles. PARSER01-CORRECTION01
       placed parseCompoundStatementInternal here.

Hard eligibility:
  semantic migration candidates MUST satisfy R0 <= 2.

R0 dominates every other ranking dimension (R1..R6). A higher-leverage
candidate (R1=0) with R0=4 loses to a less-leverage candidate (R1=3)
with R0=0.

Why R0 is first:
  PARSER01's HALT_DEPENDENCY_EXPLOSION proved that a whole-function
  candidate can be both strategically attractive and structurally
  unbridgeable. R0 is the formal expression of "structurally
  unbridgeable"; it precedes semantic leverage deliberately.
