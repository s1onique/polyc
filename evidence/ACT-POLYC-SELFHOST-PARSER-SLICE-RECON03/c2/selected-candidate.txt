--- what the function is ---
parseGetPriority maps a PUNCT TOKEN VALUE to the operator
precedence level used by the recursive-descent expression parser.
It is the operator precedence table of the whole compiler.

--- why it is the right target ---
1. The input projection is MECHANICALLY COMPLETE on ONE scalar.
   The body of src/prslib.c:1139..1207 is a single switch over
   tok->i64 and references no other field and no other identifier.
   A grep over the range finds exactly one field access (tok->i64).
2. There is NO host state: no Cctrl, no AST, no List/Map/Vec traversal, no
   pointer identity, no allocation, no callback, no recovery, no global
   or static state, and no diagnostic ownership.
3. It is MAXIMALLY production-reachable: the single decision site sits
   inside parseExpr, which has 32 production call sites across three
   files, including the macro preprocessor.
4. It is SEMANTICALLY CENTRAL: the precedence value decides the AST
   shape of every expression in every compiled program, so the
   production-causal mutation is guaranteed to be observable.
5. It UNLOCKS MULTIPLE FOLLOWING SURFACES (R1=1): the precedence table
   gates every operator classification, the associativity decision
   and the short-circuit structure of && and ||.

--- what it is NOT ---
It is NOT a generic numerical helper: it is a semantic table whose
entries are the C operator precedence classes, and whose result
changes the compiled program.
