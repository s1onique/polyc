ACT-POLYC-LLVM-SPIKE01-RESUME01-CORRECTION01 / RED-1A analysis

This file documents an honest discrepancy between RED-1A as
written in the ACT and what the source actually shows at the
--dump-ir snapshot. Per F13 and F4, this is reported as a
HALT-conditional finding rather than silently absorbed.

## 1. The literal RED-1A claim

The ACT's RED-1A says:

  expected: the dumped IR (the shape above the LLVM consumer)
            contains an IR_CMP_BR instruction
  today:    observed in current source — the pipeline emits
            IR_CMP_BR, not the canonical IR_ICMP + IR_BR pair

## 2. What the dump actually shows

Source: src/tests/llvm-spike/04_cmp_branch.HC
Command: ./hcc --dump-ir src/tests/llvm-spike/04_cmp_branch.HC

See red-1A.dump-ir.txt for the full transcript.

Key lines (after "After basic optimisations"):

    cmp_gt   %t6 i64 tmp, %p1 i64 param, %p3 i64 param  ; line 3
    br       %t6 i64 tmp, bb3, bb4  ; line 3

The textual mnemonic for IR_CMP_BR is `cmp_br_<kind>`
(src/ir-debug.c:418, 506-517). It does NOT appear in the dump.

## 3. Why the fusion is invisible to --dump-ir

src/ir.c:3301-3315 defines `irDump`:

    void irDump(Cctrl *cc) {
        ...
        irLowerFunction(ctx, ast);
        irPrintFunction(fn);
        irBasicFunctionOptimisations(fn);
        printf("===== After basic optimisations ===== \n");
        irPrintFunction(fn);
    }

`irDump` calls `irBasicFunctionOptimisations`, NOT
`irOptPinResultReg`. The latter is the only producer of
IR_CMP_BR (src/ir-optimise.c:1079-1122; src/ir-types.h:140-150).
`irOptPinResultReg` is called from
`irFunctionPrepForCodeGen` (src/ir.c:3085-3095), which is
the codegen prep path used by the native backend and the
LLVM consumer, but NOT by the dump pipeline.

## 4. RED-1A status

RED-1A as literally written DOES NOT REPRODUCE at the
--dump-ir snapshot. The dump pipeline is a witness of the
neutral IR above the boundary, and the neutral IR is the
two-instruction IR_ICMP + IR_BR form as expected.

The fusion happens between the dump snapshot and the LLVM
consumer code path. The LLVM consumer therefore DOES see
IR_CMP_BR; that is proven by RED-1B (the consumer's
IR_CMP_BR switch arm at src/llvm-backend.c:610-639
successfully emits `icmp sgt` + `br i1` for 04_cmp_branch.HC,
and the emitted .ll is verifier-clean).

## 5. What this means for IR-BOUNDARY03

The placement defect is real but slightly different from
the predecessor's literal RED-1A wording:

  - The neutral IR (above the boundary) is the canonical
    IR_ICMP + IR_BR form. This is what every backend-neutral
    analysis tool sees. Good.

  - The codegen-prep IR (below the boundary, what the
    LLVM consumer and the native codegen see) is the fused
    IR_CMP_BR form, produced by an architectural
    transformation that the neutral-IR spec does not
    authorise.

  - The LLVM consumer must silently re-translate the
    fused form back into LLVM's native icmp+condbr (its
    IR_CMP_BR arm at src/llvm-backend.c:610-639).

The defect is the silent transformation between neutral
and below-neutral IR, not the presence of IR_CMP_BR in
the neutral IR itself. The reviewer's intuition that
"IR_CMP_BR is below-boundary" (src/ir-types.h:140-150) is
literally true; what is missing is an explicit boundary
event the dump pipeline can witness.

## 6. C1 outcome

This is still `HALT_LLVM_SEES_NATIVE_FUSION`. The cause
statement in the HANDOFF is updated:

  ORIGINAL: "RED-1A reproduces — the neutral IR pipeline
             emits IR_CMP_BR for comparison sources."

  REVISED:  "The codegen-prep IR (below the neutral
             boundary) fuses IR_ICMP + IR_BR into IR_CMP_BR
             without an above-boundary witness, and the
             LLVM consumer silently re-translates the fused
             form. RED-1A's literal claim does not reproduce
             at the --dump-ir snapshot because --dump-ir is
             above the boundary. The defect is real, the
             halt is mandatory, and ACT-POLYC-IR-BOUNDARY03
             owns the fix."

The C1 close is still the expected halt. The architectural
finding strengthens rather than weakens.
