ACT-POLYC-BOOTSTRAP03 — C3 — $-identifier binding slice
=======================================================

Per ACT §21: the corpus is filtered for sources that
contain `$`-identifiers. Stage1 and stage2 must produce
byte-identical artifacts for these sources because the
B1 component is the entity responsible for accepting the
`$` continuation rule.

------------------------------------------------------------

```text
$ awk -F'\t' 'NR>1 && $2=="1"' \
    evidence/ACT-POLYC-BOOTSTRAP03/c3/corpus-matrix-stage1-stage2.tsv
```

------------------------------------------------------------
$-identifier sources (3 sources)
------------------------------------------------------------

```text
src/holyc-lib/dir.HC    has_dollar=1
src/tests/30_fzf.HC     has_dollar=1
src/tests/run_shared.HC has_dollar=1
```

All three contain real `$`-identifiers (e.g.
`_opendir$INODE64` in dir.HC) that would fail under
the B0 grammar `[A-Za-z_][A-Za-z0-9_]*` but pass under
the production grammar `[A-Za-z_][A-Za-z0-9_$]*` that the
B1 component implements.

------------------------------------------------------------
Mandatory $-source dynamic delegation
------------------------------------------------------------

```text
src/holyc-lib/dir.HC contains:
  public _extern _opendir$INODE64 cDIR *opendir(U8 *dirname);
  public _extern _readdir$INODE64 Dirent *readdir(cDIR *dir);
  public _extern _opendir         cDIR *opendir(U8 *dirname);
```

Stage2's successful compile of dir.HC proves that the
B1 component correctly scanned `_opendir$INODE64` as a
single identifier span (the production grammar accepts
`$` in continuation; the B0 grammar would not have).

If the B1 component were absent or broken in stage2,
the lexer would have terminated the identifier at the
`_` preceding `$INODE64`, leaving `$INODE64` as a stray
token, which would then fail at the parser stage. The
successful compile proves the delegation fired.
